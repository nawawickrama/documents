# Music and audio

Background music is where most claims and strikes come from. The rule is simple: use only audio we can prove we are licensed to use in a **monetised** YouTube video, and log it.

## 1. Sources by risk

**Safe (use by default)**

- **YouTube Audio Library** (in YouTube Studio). YouTube calls downloaded tracks "copyright-safe": they will not be claimed through Content ID and can be used in monetised videos.
- **Music we made ourselves** with no samples we do not own. **Terrachart's default:** `~/Documents/YouTube/Studio/scripts/music.py` writes an original ambient score in code (pad, bass and bells, pure synthesis). The channel owns it outright, so there is nothing to license and nothing for Content ID to match. Give each long video its own track (`python3 scripts/music.py 02 600`, plus a different key, tempo or seed). Credit line: "Music: original score by Terrachart".

**Fine with care (needs an active licence and proof)**

- **Paid libraries** such as Epidemic Sound, Artlist, Musicbed, Soundstripe or Uppbeat's paid plan. The channel must be on the plan's allowlist (often called safelisting or clearing the channel), and the video must be **published while the subscription is active**.
- **Creative Commons tracks with a licence that allows commercial use** (CC0, CC BY, CC BY-SA), with the exact credit the licence asks for.
- **AI music** from a service whose current terms give commercial rights on our plan (for example Suno's paid plans). Disclose it; see section 5.

**Do not use**

- Any commercial song: chart music, film or TV music, game soundtracks, covers, remixes, "slowed and reverb" versions, even a few seconds.
- Music from "no copyright music" channels or free sites with no clear licence. YouTube's own page says it is not responsible for "royalty-free" music from other sources, and these tracks are often registered in Content ID by someone.
- Anything licensed **NonCommercial** (CC BY-NC, CC BY-NC-SA, CC BY-NC-ND). Ad revenue makes a monetised video commercial use.
- Free-plan output from AI music tools whose free plan is non-commercial (Suno's free plan is). Songs made on a free plan stay non-commercial even if we upgrade later.
- Udio downloads: after its 2025 label settlements Udio stopped downloads; check its current terms before even considering it.

## 2. The YouTube Audio Library

- Find it in YouTube Studio, left menu, Audio Library. New tracks arrive twice a month.
- Filter by **"Attribution not required"** to avoid any credit work.
- For **"Attribution required"** tracks (Creative Commons): click the licence icon, copy the credit text, and paste it into the description exactly. The "Music in this video" panel YouTube adds does **not** replace this credit.
- Note the track name and licence type in the licence log.
- Editing (cutting, looping, fading) is fine.
- YouTube's page talks about use on YouTube. Do not assume a track is cleared for TikTok, Instagram or anywhere else.

## 3. Paid music libraries

- Connect or register the channel in the service's dashboard before publishing (Epidemic Sound calls it safelisting).
- Videos published while the subscription is active stay cleared permanently (Epidemic Sound's own wording). Videos published after it ends will be claimed.
- Download each track from our account, so the licence is tied to us.
- If a claim still arrives, dispute it within five days with proof of the active subscription and allowlisting, so ad revenue is not lost in the gap.
- Log the plan name, the date and the track ID.

## 4. Creative Commons and "free" music

| Licence | Monetised YouTube? | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| CC0 / public domain | Yes | Log the source page. |
| CC BY | Yes | Credit as the licence says: title, artist, licence, link. |
| CC BY-SA | Yes, with care | Credit; the share-alike clause can apply to our video. Prefer another track. |
| CC BY-ND | Usually yes | Credit; do not edit the track itself. Prefer another track. |
| Any **NC** licence | **No** | Do not use. |
| "Free for YouTube" with no written licence | **No** | Do not use. |

Public domain *compositions* (Bach, Beethoven) are free, but a particular *recording* usually is not. Only use a recording that is itself public domain or licensed to us.

## 5. AI-generated music

- Only from a plan whose current terms grant commercial use, at the time we generate it. Save a screenshot of the plan and terms with the date in the licence log.
- YouTube's altered or synthetic content page lists **AI-generated music** among things to disclose. When we use AI music, set "AI use" to **Yes** at upload. Disclosure does not reduce reach or monetisation.
- AI music is not protected by copyright in many places, so someone else could reuse it. And similar-sounding AI tracks have been claimed by mistake. The Audio Library avoids both problems.
- Never ask an AI tool to imitate a named artist's voice or a specific song.

## 6. Music in Shorts

- Songs added in the YouTube Shorts editor from its music list are licensed for Shorts. Each partner-music track reduces that Short's share of the creator pool (one track: half; two tracks: a third), but not our 45 percent cut of what is allocated. Using Audio Library music or none keeps the whole share.
- Those Shorts-editor songs are not licensed for long-form videos. Do not reuse them there.
- A Short over one minute that contains claimed content is blocked worldwide.
- Do not add a commercial song in an outside editor and upload it as a Short. It will be claimed or blocked.

## 7. Myths that get channels struck

- "Giving credit makes it legal." No. Credit is only enough when the licence says so.
- "Under 10, 15 or 30 seconds is fine." No such rule exists.
- "No copyright infringement intended" in the description. Means nothing.
- "Fair use." A US legal defence decided case by case, not a permission. Background music under a chart is not commentary on that music, so it is not fair use.
- "I bought the song / I have Spotify Premium." A personal listening licence is not a sync licence for video.
- "Pitch-shift or speed it up so Content ID misses it." This is detection evasion under the spam policy, and songs altered only in pitch or speed are named as reused content.
- "It's only a claim, not a strike." A claim can take all the ad revenue and, if disputed wrongly, become a strike.

## 8. Sound effects

Same rules. The Audio Library has sound effects. Other sources need a licence that allows commercial use. Log them. Do not lift sounds from films, games or TV.

## 9. Mixing and use

- Music sits under the voice (roughly −18 to −24 dB below narration; duck it while the voice speaks).
- Vary tracks between videos. One track on every video is a mass-production signal.
- Keep the original downloaded files and their licence pages with the project, not just the final render.
