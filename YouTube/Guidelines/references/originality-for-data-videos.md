# Originality for data videos

This is the policy most likely to stop our channel. Read it before planning a series, and again before the YPP review.

## 1. What the policies say

YouTube's channel monetisation policies have two rules that matter here. Both apply to the whole channel.

**Inauthentic content** (renamed from "repetitious content" on 15 July 2025). Not allowed: content that is repetitive or mass-produced, with low educational value or minimal variation between videos; image slideshows, templated storylines or scrolling text with little narrative; AI-generated content made from generic templates without the creator's own insight. Allowed: the same intro and outro across videos when the main content differs; a series where each video has its own focus or concept.

**Reused content.** Not allowed: material from YouTube or elsewhere repurposed without significant original commentary, substantial changes, or educational or entertainment value. Examples that fail: readings of other people's material such as website or news text; content uploaded many times by others; compilations. Permission from the original owner does not make reused content eligible.

The spam policy adds: using automated tools or AI to mass-produce similar content with minimal changes is not allowed. Its own example is channels reusing the same background music and repetitive AI imagery across many videos with AI-written scripts.

**AI personas on sensitive topics:** a channel whose AI persona presents itself as a human expert on health, legal, financial or political topics is not eligible to monetise.

YouTube has said it judges the output, not the tool. AI help is allowed. A channel that looks like it came off a template is not.

## 2. Why data channels get refused

A bar chart race on its own is exactly the pattern these policies describe: the same template, data anyone can download, music, no narration, a new upload every day. Thousands of channels make the same "Top 10 GDP 1960–2025" video. A reviewer who opens five of our videos and sees the same template with different numbers will refuse the channel, and the appeal will fail because there is nothing to show.

The fix is not to stop making data videos. It is to make the data the material and the story the product.

## 3. How we make every video clearly ours

Every video needs all of these:

- **A question or claim of our own.** Not "Top 10 economies", but "Why did South Korea pass the UK in exports?" The title and the narration answer it.
- **Narration that explains, in our words.** Why the leader changed in 1998, what the dip in 2009 was, why one country is missing before 1991. The voice should be telling the viewer things the moving bars cannot.
- **Our own data work.** Combining two sources, adjusting for inflation or population, choosing a fairer measure, spotting an error in the raw data. Say on screen what we did ("per person, in 2024 dollars").
- **Context on screen.** Event labels on the timeline, annotations, a pause to compare two lines, a map, a closing chart that sums up.
- **A conclusion.** End with what it means, not just the final frame.
- **Sources named** on screen and in the description.

Signals of effort the reviewer can see in the video itself: a structure with an intro, sections and an ending; chapters in the description; changing chart types within a video; a voice that refers to what is on screen at that moment.

The script can be drafted with AI, but every script must be reviewed and rewritten with real analysis. Never generate a batch of near-identical scripts from one prompt with different numbers.

## 4. Formats: what to vary

Keep a recognisable brand (colours, fonts, intro, logo), but vary the content:

- **Chart types:** bar race, line race, map over time, bubble chart, stacked area, before/after comparisons, a single big number explained.
- **Structures:** countdown, head-to-head, "how X changed", myth versus data, one country's story, a prediction checked against what happened.
- **Lengths:** long-form 8 to 20 minutes; Shorts 20 to 60 seconds (up to 3 minutes is allowed).
- **Music:** different tracks across videos, chosen for the mood. The same track on every video is one of the named mass-production signals.
- **Topics:** a balance of themes within the channel's main subject, so the channel has a clear identity without being a copy machine.

Upload pace: quality over count. Three good long-form videos a week with Shorts cut from them beats a daily template.

## 5. Shorts

- Since 1 October 2026, Shorts recommendations favour original content and reduce the reach of channels that mostly re-upload others' work. Our own Shorts, including clips from our own long-form videos, are original.
- A Short should carry one idea with narration or on-screen text that explains it, not just 30 seconds of moving bars.
- Views on non-original Shorts are not counted for Shorts revenue at all.
- Do not re-upload the same Short twice, mirror it, change its speed or pitch, or post it on several channels. That is the detection-evasion and mass-production behaviour the spam policy names.

## 6. Self-audit before review

Do this when the channel nears 1,000 subscribers and the hours or views threshold. Look at the channel as a stranger would.

1. **Home page and About:** does the About section say who makes it, what the channel is about and where the data comes from? Is there a clear theme?
2. **Most-viewed five videos:** each has narration with analysis, sources named, a title that matches the content.
3. **Newest five videos:** same test. Reviewers look at the newest ones, so do not let quality drop while waiting.
4. **Side by side:** open any three videos. Would a stranger say they are clearly different videos with different ideas, or one template with different numbers?
5. **Titles and thumbnails:** nothing misleading, nothing that only works because it shocks.
6. **Music:** not the same track everywhere; every track in the licence log.
7. **Anything weak:** make it private before the review (private and unlisted videos do not count for the hours, so think about the hours lost).
8. **Evidence for an appeal:** keep screen recordings of the data work, the script drafts and the edit. If refused, the appeal video is mostly ready.
9. **An old channel we revived:** reviewers see the whole channel, not only the new videos. Make private every old video that is off-theme, low effort or not entirely ours (re-uploads, clips, other people's music) before the review. Only public long-form hours from the last 12 months count, so old videos add little to the hours anyway.
