# Claims, strikes and limited ads

Different notices mean very different things. Read which one it is before acting.

## Contents

1. Content ID claim
2. Copyright strike
3. Community Guidelines warning and strikes
4. Limited or no ads (yellow or red icon)
5. Monetisation suspended or YPP refused

## 1. Content ID claim

**What it is:** an automatic match against a rights holder's reference file. It is **not** a strike and does not harm the channel's standing on its own.

**What it does:** the claimant's policy decides: take the ad revenue, track viewing, or block the video in some or all countries.

**What to do:**

1. Open YouTube Studio, Content, the video, and read the claim: which track, which seconds, who claims it.
2. Check the licence log.
   - **We have a licence** (Audio Library, paid library on an allowlist, our own music): dispute it, attach the proof, and do it within five days so ad revenue is held instead of lost.
   - **We do not have a licence:** do not dispute. Use Studio's editor to trim the section, replace the song with an Audio Library track, or mute the song. That releases the claim. Add the source to the "do not use" list.
3. Never dispute just to see what happens. If the claimant rejects the dispute and we appeal without a real basis, they can file a takedown, and that becomes a copyright strike.

## 2. Copyright strike

**What it is:** the video was removed after a legal copyright takedown request.

**Effects:**

- One or two strikes: the video is removed. The strike expires after 90 days, but only if we complete Copyright School (four questions, done once).
- **Three active strikes: the account and every linked channel are subject to termination**, all videos are removed and we cannot create new channels.
- Live streaming is restricted for 7 days after a live-stream strike, 14 days after a second.

**What to do:**

1. If YouTube gives a "scheduled removal" (7 days' notice), deleting or editing the video within those 7 days avoids the strike. Act on it the same day.
2. If we had no licence: complete Copyright School and wait the 90 days. Remove the source from our workflow.
3. If we had a licence: contact the claimant with proof and ask them to retract. A counter notification is a legal step under US law (our name and address go to the claimant, who can then sue). Use it only with a real licence or real fair use, and get advice first.
4. Do not upload more risky content while a strike is active.

## 3. Community Guidelines warning and strikes

- **First violation:** usually a warning, with no other penalty. Doing the policy training lets it expire after 90 days. A second violation within that window brings a strike.
- **Strike 1:** one week without uploading. **Strike 2** within 90 days: two weeks. **Strike 3** within 90 days: the channel is terminated.
- Each strike expires 90 days after it was given.
- **Any active strike blocks a YPP application.**
- Appeal from Studio if we believe it is a mistake, with a short factual explanation.

## 4. Limited or no ads (yellow or red icon)

- Yellow means limited ads; red means no ads. Usually caused by the topic, the title or the thumbnail (see `metadata-and-community.md`).
- If we think it is wrong: request human review in Studio. Do it early, ideally before the video gets most of its views (upload as unlisted, wait for the check, then publish).
- Often a change of title or thumbnail fixes it. Editing the video file itself is not possible after upload.

## 5. Monetisation suspended or YPP refused

See `monetization-and-ypp.md` section 4. In short: read the named policy; appeal within 21 days with a video showing how we make our content, or fix the channel and reapply after 30 days. Never open a new channel to get around it.
