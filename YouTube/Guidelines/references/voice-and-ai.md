# Voice and AI

AI tools are allowed on YouTube. What gets channels refused is AI used to mass-produce near-identical videos, AI pretending to be a human expert on sensitive topics, and realistic AI content passed off as real.

## 1. Voiceover options

Best for the channel, in order:

1. **The owner's own voice.** The strongest signal of an authentic channel, and nothing to license.
2. **A clone of the owner's own voice**, made with the owner's consent on a paid plan. YouTube says cloning your own voice for voiceovers needs no AI disclosure.
3. **A stock text-to-speech voice** from a service whose terms allow commercial use on the plan we pay for.

**Free option we use: Kokoro-82M.** It runs on the Mac (via `kokoro-js`, already installed in `~/Desktop/Developments/coredile-pos-videos`). Model and voices are Apache 2.0, trained only on public-domain or permissively licensed audio, and the model card welcomes production and commercial use. That makes it fine for monetised videos. Best voices: `af_heart` (American, grade A), `af_bella` (American, A-), `bf_emma` (British, B-, the best British one). **Terrachart uses `af_heart`** (the owner's choice, 2 November 2026); keep the same voice on every video so the channel sounds like one show. Log it once in the licence log as "Kokoro-82M, Apache 2.0", and put "Narration: AI voice (Kokoro)" in the description.

**Never the Mac's built-in `say` voices** (Daniel, Samantha and the rest) in a monetised video. Apple's macOS licence allows them for personal, non-commercial projects only.

Rules for any text-to-speech service:

- **The plan must include a commercial licence at the time we generate the audio.** ElevenLabs' own help centre says the free plan has no commercial licence and cannot be used commercially; its paid plans (from Starter) do. Audio generated on a free plan stays non-commercial.
- Read the service's terms once and log them (screenshot with date). Some services require telling listeners the voice is AI (OpenAI's text-to-speech terms do). A line in the description such as "Narration: AI voice (service name)" satisfies that and costs nothing.
- Keep the generated audio files and their dates; that is our licence evidence.
- Vary delivery. The same flat voice reading the same script structure on every video adds to the mass-produced look. Write scripts that sound spoken, with pauses and emphasis.

## 2. Voices we must never use

- A clone or imitation of any real person other than the owner: celebrities, politicians, presenters, other YouTubers, a famous documentary narrator. This breaks the service's terms, can break the person's rights, and YouTube's privacy rules let people request removal of AI content that simulates their face or voice.
- Library "celebrity sound-alike" voices.
- Any voice used to make a real person appear to say something they did not.

## 3. AI personas and sensitive topics

YouTube's monetisation policy: channels that use AI personas presenting themselves as human experts on **health, legal, financial or political** topics are not eligible to monetise. Examples given: an AI "doctor", AI podcast hosts giving financial guidance, an AI persona giving legal advice.

Our data videos touch money (GDP, wealth, company values) and politics (elections, population). Stay safe by:

- Never giving the narrator a fake human identity, a name with credentials, or a face, or calling it an "expert", "analyst" or "advisor".
- Presenting data, not advice. "Here is how the top economies changed" is fine; "You should invest in X" is not.
- Putting a short line in finance-related descriptions: "For information only. Not financial advice."
- Staying neutral and sourced on political data. Election data must match official results; election misinformation is removed under Community Guidelines.
- Following health authorities on health data. Medical misinformation (contradicting local health authorities or the WHO) is against Community Guidelines and gets no ads.

## 4. AI for scripts, images and video

**Scripts.** AI-assisted outlines and scripts need no disclosure. But every script must contain our own analysis and be rewritten by us. Generating many scripts from one template prompt is the mass-production pattern. Check every fact the AI writes against the data; AI invents numbers.

**Images and thumbnails.** AI images for thumbnails, backgrounds and illustrations are allowed. They must not mislead: no realistic AI image of a real person doing something they did not do, no fake news-style photos, no realistic fake disaster scenes.

**Video.** Short AI clips can decorate a video, but stitched AI clips with no narrative arc are named in the policy as not monetisable. The chart and the narration are the content; AI footage is at most garnish.

## 5. The "altered or synthetic content" setting at upload

In YouTube Studio, under the upload details, answer the AI use question.

**Answer Yes when the video contains realistic content that a viewer could take as real**, made or changed with AI, including:

- a real person shown saying or doing something they did not;
- changed footage of a real event or place;
- a realistic scene that never happened (for example a realistic weather event moving toward a real city);
- AI-generated music (YouTube lists it among the examples).

**No disclosure needed for:**

- charts, infographics and animation that are clearly not real footage;
- AI help with outlines, scripts, titles, thumbnails or ideas;
- cloning the owner's own voice for narration;
- colour, lighting, upscaling, captions and similar small edits;
- a stock synthetic narration voice that is not imitating a real person (YouTube's page does not list it; when unsure, say Yes, since disclosure does not reduce reach or monetisation).

Not disclosing when we should can lead to YouTube adding the label itself, removal of content, or suspension from YPP. Disclosing never hurts. When in doubt, disclose.
