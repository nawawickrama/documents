# Data and visuals

Numbers are our raw material. They need to be right, allowed, and credited. The same goes for every flag, photo, logo, map and font on screen.

## 1. Choosing a data source

Facts themselves (a country's population in 2020) are not owned by anyone. But a website's dataset, its tables, its charts and its terms of use can still restrict what we do with them. So:

- Prefer open sources with a written licence that allows commercial use (most international organisations and national statistics offices).
- Use paywalled or proprietary sources (Statista, Bloomberg, Forbes lists, research firms) only to check a fact, or quote a single figure with credit. Do not rebuild their whole table or chart into a video.
- Never screen-record another creator's chart or video, or download a ready-made bar chart race. That is reused content and possibly copyright infringement.
- Write the source, the licence and the download date into the licence log.

## 2. Sources and their licences

| Source | Licence | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| World Bank Open Data | CC BY 4.0 by default; commercial use allowed | Some datasets carry other licences; check the dataset page. Do not imply the World Bank endorses us or use its logo. |
| Our World in Data | CC BY for what OWID produces | Much of OWID's data comes from others (WHO, UN, World Bank) and keeps those providers' terms. Credit both OWID and the original source. |
| IMF data | Free to use with attribution under its terms for published statistics | Say if we changed the data materially. Check the IMF copyright page for the current wording. |
| UN data (UN Data, UN DESA population) | Free reuse with citation | Check the specific dataset's terms. |
| Eurostat, OECD, national statistics offices | Mostly open with attribution | Check each one. |
| Wikipedia | Text CC BY-SA | Fine for finding facts; trace each number back to Wikipedia's own cited source and cite that. Do not read Wikipedia text aloud. |
| Google Trends | Use with attribution "Source: Google Trends" | Values are relative (0–100), not search counts. Say so. |
| Paid or proprietary (Statista, Bloomberg, Forbes) | Restricted | Single facts with credit only. |

## 3. Citing a source

- **On screen:** a small, readable line during the chart, e.g. "Source: World Bank (2025), GDP, current US$".
- **In the description:** a "Sources" section with the full name, dataset, year and link, plus what we did to the data ("adjusted for inflation to 2024 US$; per person using UN population").
- If the licence requires specific attribution wording, use it exactly.

## 4. Accuracy

Wrong numbers lose trust and, on health or election topics, can break the misinformation rules.

- Check every number in the script against the downloaded data. AI-written scripts often invent or misremember figures.
- Label estimates, projections and gaps ("data before 1990 is estimated", "no data for 2003").
- Say what the measure is: nominal or real, total or per person, which currency and year.
- Use the latest official figures for elections, and health authority data for health topics.
- When data is disputed, say so instead of picking a side.
- Keep the cleaned dataset with the project so any number can be traced.

## 5. Flags, logos, photos and maps

**Flags.** National flags are generally free to use. Use clean versions (for example public-domain SVGs on Wikimedia Commons, checking each file's licence). Never alter or deface a flag; it offends viewers and some countries have laws about it.

**Logos.** Company and brand logos are trademarks and the artwork is often copyrighted. Showing a logo to identify a company in a ranking is common practice, but:

- prefer the company name in text with a brand colour, or the logo from the company's own press kit;
- never suggest the company sponsors or endorses us;
- never put a brand logo in a way that looks like an advert or makes fun of the brand.

**Photos of people and places.** Only from a source with a licence we can show: our own, Wikimedia Commons (check each file's licence and credit it), Unsplash, Pexels or Pixabay (their licences allow commercial use; read the restrictions on identifiable people), or a paid stock library. Never images from Google Images, news sites, Instagram or other YouTube videos. Do not use a real person's photo in a way that suggests they endorse us.

**Maps.**

- Map shapes: Natural Earth (public domain) is safe. OpenStreetMap data needs "© OpenStreetMap contributors".
- Google Maps or Google Earth imagery has its own attribution rules; follow Google's permissions page or avoid it.
- Borders are sensitive (Kashmir, Taiwan, Crimea, Western Sahara and others). Use a neutral convention, mark disputed areas as disputed, and expect comments either way. Do not make a map that takes a political side by accident.

## 6. Fonts

- Google Fonts are under the SIL Open Font License or similar and are free for use in videos.
- A font that came with an app or the operating system, or a commercial font, may not cover video use. Check, or use Google Fonts.
- Log the fonts once per channel (name and licence), not per video.
