# Titles, thumbnails, descriptions and community rules

Metadata is part of the content. YouTube's advertiser-friendly guidelines and spam policy both cover titles, thumbnails, descriptions and tags, and the YPP review looks at them directly.

## 1. Titles and thumbnails

**Allowed and effective:** curiosity, a surprising number, a question the video really answers, a clear comparison ("India vs China: 75 years of GDP").

**Not allowed:**

- A title or thumbnail that promises something the video does not show ("malicious clickbait" under the spam policy). If the thumbnail shows the number 1, the video must show who is number 1.
- Strong profanity in a title or thumbnail (no ads). Moderate profanity there limits ads.
- Sexual, shocking, gory or violent imagery in a thumbnail, even if the video is clean.
- Realistic AI images of real people doing things they did not do, or fake "breaking news" images.
- Other creators' thumbnails or images we have no licence for.

**Good practice:** keep a consistent brand look, use large readable text (three to five words), make sure the claim on the thumbnail is the one the video pays off.

## 2. Descriptions, tags and hashtags

Every long-form description should have:

- two or three lines saying what the video shows and why it matters;
- chapters (timestamps), which help viewers and show structure;
- a **Sources** section (see `data-and-visuals.md`);
- a **Music** section with every attribution a licence requires;
- "Narration: AI voice (service)" if we used text-to-speech;
- "For information only. Not financial advice." on finance-related videos.

Tags and hashtags:

- Only tags that describe this video. Lists of unrelated or trending keywords are misleading metadata.
- A few hashtags (three is plenty). YouTube ignores all hashtags on a video that has too many (more than 60), and irrelevant hashtags count as misleading.
- No other channels' names as tags to borrow their audience.

The About section of the channel: who makes it (it can be a brand name), what the channel covers, where the data comes from, how often we upload. Reviewers read it.

## 3. Made for kids

- Every video and the channel default must be set. It is a legal requirement under COPPA in the US, and wrong settings can bring YouTube penalties and legal consequences.
- **Our data videos are "No, not made for kids."** General-audience animation that appeals to everyone is not made for kids. Being family friendly does not make it made for kids.
- It becomes "made for kids" if it is aimed mainly at children: child-focused characters, nursery-style songs, early learning, kid-targeted titles ("Learn countries for kids!"). Avoid making videos like that on this channel; made-for-kids videos lose personalised ads, comments and more, and have their own quality rules.

## 4. Topics that limit ads

Data channels often touch these. We can make the video; know the cost. Video, title, thumbnail, description and tags are all judged.

| Topic | Usually |
|---|---|
| War deaths, casualties, conflicts | Limited ads. No ads if it exploits or glorifies. Raw conflict footage limits ads; do not use it. |
| Disasters, terrorism, mass violence | Full ads if discussed respectfully. No ads if it profits from a recent tragedy (event names as clickbait). |
| Crime rates, murder rates | Usually fine as statistics; graphic imagery limits ads. |
| Suicide, self-harm, eating disorders | Limited ads at best. Add help-line information. Usually better avoided. |
| Drugs, alcohol, tobacco | Educational data is fine; anything that promotes use limits or removes ads. |
| Guns | Neutral statistics are fine; promotion or modification removes ads. |
| Profanity | Occasional mild words are fine; frequent or strong profanity limits ads. Keep narration clean. |
| Sexual content | Do not. |

The monetisation icon in YouTube Studio: green is full ads, yellow is limited, red is none. If a video gets yellow and we think it is wrong, request a human review from Studio. Repeated unsuitable content can turn ads off for the whole channel.

## 5. Engagement and comments

- Asking viewers to like, comment and subscribe is fine.
- Not allowed: buying views, subscribers, likes, comments or watch time; "promotion" services that guarantee views; sub-for-sub or like-for-like groups; offering rewards for subscribing; giveaways that require subscribing. These break the fake engagement and spam policies, the views do not count, and they can end the channel.
- Google Ads (real paid advertising through YouTube) is allowed, but its views are not public organic views and they help little toward watch hours.
- Do not post "check out my channel" comments on other videos, in bulk or not.
- Pinned comments, polls and community posts are fine, if they are not repetitive low-effort engagement bait.

## 6. Community Guidelines that matter to a data channel

- **Misinformation:** elections (results, how to vote), health (contradicting local health authorities or the WHO), and manipulated content that misleads about real events. Use official data and say when something is disputed.
- **Hate and harassment:** rankings of countries, religions, ethnic groups or real people must not demean them. "Most violent religion" or "ugliest nationality" style videos break the rules and lose ads. Compare data, not worth.
- **Privacy:** do not show private individuals' personal details. Public figures' public data (net worth, election results) is fine.
- **Sensitive events:** no videos that profit from a fresh tragedy.
- **Spam and deceptive practices:** misleading metadata, repetitive mass-produced uploads, the same content on several channels.

The first Community Guidelines violation usually brings a warning (removable by doing the policy training; it expires after 90 days). After that come strikes. Any active strike blocks a YPP application. See `claims-and-strikes.md`.
